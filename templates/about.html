Project Methodology & Clinical Limitations

An in-depth explanation of the deep learning architecture, computer vision preprocessing pipeline, training strategy, and critical ethical and clinical limitations essential for Minor Project defense.

End-to-End System Architecture

From raw dermatoscopic image to clinical decision support

01

Image Ingestion & Verification

Uploaded files are validated using the Python Imaging Library (PIL). The module checks file headers, verifies that pixel data is not truncated or corrupted, and converts grayscale, RGBA, or CMYK images into a uniform 3-channel RGB color space.

02

Spatial Resizing & Normalization

Dermatoscopic images vary greatly in capture resolution (e.g., 600×450 px). Images are resized to a standard 224 × 224 pixel tensor using high-fidelity Lanczos resampling. Raw integer pixel intensities in [0, 255] are rescaled into float32 values in [0.0, 1.0] to ensure numerical stability and prevent gradient explosion in deep neural layers.

03

Data Augmentation Pipeline

To combat overfitting on minority classes (e.g., Dermatofibroma with only 115 samples), a TensorFlow Keras Sequential augmentation layer applies:

  • Random Horizontal & Vertical Flips: Dermatological lesions have radial/circular symmetry.
  • Random Rotations (±10%): Simulates varying camera angles during dermoscopy.
  • Random Zoom (±10%): Simulates lens magnification distance variance.
  • Contrast Variation (±10%): Robustness against varying clinical light sources.
04

Deep Learning Backbone (Transfer Learning)

Rather than training a deep network from scratch on limited medical samples, the project utilizes Transfer Learning. Architectures such as MobileNetV2, EfficientNetB0, or ResNet50 pre-trained on ImageNet provide rich hierarchical edge, texture, and pattern representations.

The convolutional base is followed by Global Average Pooling, a Dropout regularization layer (rate = 0.3), and a Dense classification head with 7 output units activated via Softmax:

$$P(Y = i \mid \mathbf{x}) = \frac{e^{z_i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{7} e^{z_j}}$$
05

Inference & Web Integration

A lightweight Flask REST API receives query images, invokes the preprocessed tensor through the model loader, and returns structured JSON containing top predicted disease, confidence percentage, and class probability distribution for client-side rendering.

Clinical Limitations & Ethical Considerations

Critical real-world medical considerations for viva examination

1. Skin Tone Representation Bias (Fitzpatrick Scale)

Benchmark datasets including HAM10000 were predominantly collected from clinics in Austria and Australia, consisting primarily of fair-skinned individuals (Fitzpatrick skin types I–III). As a consequence, deep learning models may exhibit degraded sensitivity or false classifications on darker skin types (IV–VI). Diverse multi-ethnic dataset curation is essential before clinical translation.

2. Dermatoscopy Artifact Sensitivity

Real-world clinical images often contain confounding artifacts such as hair follicles, air bubbles from immersion oil, purple surgical ink markings, and physical ruler calibration marks. Convolutions can inadvertently latch onto ruler markings as high-risk features if ruler marks were disproportionately present near malignant lesions in the training data (Clever Hans effect).

3. Asymmetry of Misclassification Costs

In computer vision benchmarks, all errors are typically weighted equally. In clinical oncology, however, a False Negative (misclassifying Malignant Melanoma as a benign nevus) can lead to delayed treatment and fatal metastasis. Conversely, a False Positive causes temporary patient anxiety and a follow-up biopsy. AI models in dermatology must be calibrated for maximum sensitivity on malignant classes.

4. Image Domain Shift (Dermatoscope vs. Smartphone Camera)

The HAM10000 dataset consists strictly of standardized dermatoscopic images taken with specialized epiluminescence devices. Feeding consumer smartphone camera photos with inconsistent illumination, shadows, and perspective distortion represents an out-of-distribution domain shift, where neural network predictions become unreliable.

5. Biopsy as Definitive Ground Truth

Even certified human dermatologists with dermoscopy achieve clinical diagnostic accuracy of 75%–85%. Histopathological examination under a microscope following an excisional biopsy remains the true gold standard for confirming malignant neoplasms. AI serves strictly as an assistive second-opinion tool.

6. Explainability & Clinician Trust (Black-Box Challenge)

Deep neural networks lack intrinsic clinical interpretability. Future phases should incorporate Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) to generate visual heatmaps showing which anatomical regions of the lesion the convolutional kernels activated upon.
